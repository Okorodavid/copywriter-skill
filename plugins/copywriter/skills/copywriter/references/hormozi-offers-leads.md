# Offers, hooks and leads (Alex Hormozi)

Notes in our own words from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads*, *$100M Money Models* and his YouTube and TikTok talks. These ideas cover **what** to offer and how to get attention. The rest of the references cover **how** to say it. Every idea here still follows the honesty rules: real proof, real limits, and [brackets] for anything unproven.

## The order of what matters

The market comes first, then the offer, then the persuasion. A hungry market saves a weak offer, and a great offer saves weak selling. If copy isn't working, check the offer before polishing the words.

## Sell the problem, not the tool

- Nobody wants to buy "AI" or "software". They want the result: the missed calls answered, the hours back.
- Ask for their number, then use it: "Roughly how many sales a month do you lose because nobody picks up?" Their own number beats any stat you bring.
- **Invisible value:** when your service working means nothing happens (IT, security, support), buyers compare on price. Talk about what one failure costs, then offer a small, controlled guarantee ("any day it's down is free").

## The value equation

Value = (dream outcome x belief it will work) / (time to result x effort and sacrifice)

- **Dream outcome:** tie it to status, meaning how their boss, clients, family or rivals will see them.
- **Belief it will work:** proof, a track record, a demo on their own data, a guarantee. People pay for certainty.
- **Time:** give a fast first win, soon after they say yes. Fast beats free.
- **Effort:** done-for-you is worth more than do-it-yourself.
- Everyone claims the dream outcome, so win on the bottom of the equation: faster and easier.
- Perception counts. Showing progress ("here's what it did today") raises value as much as the progress itself.

## Building an offer

1. Write the dream outcome.
2. List every problem the buyer meets before, during and after using you. For each one, check the four doubts: not worth it, won't work for me, too hard, takes too long.
3. Turn each problem into a solution, often worded "how to ... even if ...".
4. List the ways to deliver each one: one-to-one or group, done-for-you or do-it-yourself, how fast you respond.
5. Cut the costly, low-value items. Keep what's high-value and cheap to deliver.
6. Leave no objection unanswered, because one unanswered "but what about X" can lose the sale.

**Offer levels.** Each level adds one more thing to the promise:
1. The outcome.
2. The outcome by a date.
3. The outcome by a date, with a guarantee.
4. The outcome by a date, with a guarantee, and done for them.

## Naming an offer: MAGIC

Use 3-5 of these parts:
- **M**agnetic reason why (launch, new season, founding clients)
- **A**vatar (who it's for)
- **G**oal (the dream outcome)
- **I**nterval (the time frame)
- **C**ontainer word (Sprint, System, Blueprint, Launch)

Example shape: "[Interval] [Goal] [Container] for [Avatar]". Name the bonuses too.

## Guarantees

- Use the shape: "If you don't get X in Y time, we'll Z." A guarantee without a "Z" is weak.
- Kinds:
  - **unconditional:** a refund, good for low-price sales to consumers;
  - **conditional:** they do the key actions and you deliver, good for high-price B2B;
  - **anti-guarantee:** all sales final, with a strong reason why;
  - **performance:** pay per result, or the second payment is due only after the first result.
- Give the guarantee a vivid name. A guarantee boosts a good offer but can't save a bad one.

## Bonuses, scarcity and urgency

- Never discount the core offer. Add a bonus that removes their stated doubt instead.
- Each bonus gets a benefit name, a reason it exists, and its real value.
- Use scarcity only when it's honest, and state your real capacity ("I take 3 new clients a month"). Urgency comes from real start dates or cohorts.
- If someone asks for a discount, offer less (for example, drop the guarantee) instead of the same thing for less.

## Hooks: proof, promise, plan

- The hook is the most important part of any ad, post or cold message.
- A strong opening gives a **proof** point, the **promise** (the result), and the **plan** (the next step).
- Reuse hooks that win. Trust the numbers over your sense of what's creative.

## Content: hook, retain, reward

- **Hook = topic + headline + format.**
  - The headline uses 2 or more of: recency, relevance, a well-known name, closeness to the reader, conflict, something unusual, or an ongoing story.
  - The format matches what already works on that platform.
- **Retain:** plant questions with lists, steps and stories.
- **Reward:** deliver what the hook promised. Nothing is too long, only too boring.
- Say "how I", not "how to". Your own story is proof, while advice is just opinion.
- People need reminding more than teaching, so repeat your best ideas.
- **Give until they ask.** Give about 3-4 times for every ask. Ask inside the content after a valuable moment, or make every 10th post an ask.
- Keep a "greatest hits" list of your best pieces to send leads before and after a call.

## Ads: callout, value, CTA

- **Callout:** the first 5 seconds decide everything. Use one of these:
  - a label ("Plumbers:");
  - a yes-question;
  - an if-then ("If you run a gym, ...");
  - a striking result;
  - a contrast;
  - someone who looks like the buyer;
  - their scene.
- **Value:** use what, who and when.
  - **What:** the dream, belief, speed and ease, or their opposites: the nightmare, the risk, the delay, the hassle.
  - **Who:** the people who give or take status from them, such as their boss, partner or rivals.
  - **When:** past, present or future.
- **CTA:** clear, not clever. Give the exact next action and a true reason to act now. Any real "because" beats none.
- Send them to a simple page that matches the ad.

## Lead magnets

- A lead magnet is a complete fix for one narrow problem that reveals the next problem, which your offer solves.
- Use one when the price is high or trust is low. "Book a call" only works on people who already know you.
- There are three kinds:
  - **reveal a problem**, like an audit or a test (the strongest kind, because the pain grows while they wait);
  - **a sample or trial**;
  - **one step of a longer process**.
- When revealing a problem, be specific about the bad and vaguer about the good. It's more believable and safer.
- Name formulas:
  - number + outcome + time ("3 emails that turn cold leads into calls in 24 hours");
  - "how to [good] without [bad] even if [worry]";
  - "the lazy [X] template that [result]";
  - "X mistakes that keep you from [goal]".
- Test the name first. The headline matters most, then the image.
- Make it really good. Give away the secrets and sell the doing.
- A plain screen-recorded case study ("how we got [result] for [client]") often beats a polished webinar.

## Cold outreach

- **Personalise:** mention 1-3 things a friend would know about them.
- **Big fast value:** give away something they'd normally pay for, like an audit, a sample or a short video on their own business. Never disguise a sales call as a "free strategy session".
- **Volume and follow-up:** follow up more times, in more ways. Email first, then "following up on my email" by phone or DM. Re-contact old lists every 3-6 months, because their situation changes.
- **When they reply,** acknowledge what they said, compliment something real, then ask a question that steers toward the problem.
- **Reviving old leads:** "Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?"
- **Asking for referrals:** "Do you know anyone who [struggle] and wants [result] in [time]?"
- Expect 2-3 conversations before a high-price sale.

## When copy stops working

Change things in this order, and keep the offer itself for last:
1. the creative (image or video);
2. the body copy;
3. the headline or name;
4. the time frame;
5. the bonus or free item;
6. the offer and how they pay.

Track every step from sent to sold (opened, replied, booked, showed up, bought), so you fix the step that's actually leaking.

## Check

- Does it sell the result, with a number from their world, and never "AI" or the tool?
- Does it win on speed and ease, not just the dream?
- Does the hook carry proof, a promise and a plan?
- Is every doubt answered, and does any guarantee follow "if not X in Y, we Z"?
- Is the CTA clear, with a true reason to act now?
- Are all the scarcity, numbers and proof real, or in [brackets]?

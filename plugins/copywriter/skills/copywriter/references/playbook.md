# Copywriting playbook

Read this first. It boils down Cialdini's *Influence* (new and expanded edition), Bly's *The Copywriter's Handbook* (4th edition), and talks by Harry Dry, Tyson 4D and Stefan Georgi, all in our own words. The other files in this folder hold the detail.

## The one rule under everything

Every line must be **true, specific and about the reader**. Run three tests on each sentence (Harry Dry):
- **Can I picture it?** "Replies in 40 seconds at 9pm" passes. "Fast, smart replies" fails.
- **Can it be proved true or false?** If it can't, it's filler. Cut it or prove it.
- **Could a competitor say the same thing?** If they could, it isn't selling this product.

Anything the user hasn't proved stays in [brackets] until it's true: numbers, results, client names, dates, reviews.

## The order every piece follows

Bly's selling sequence, with Georgi's checklist folded in:
1. **Get attention** with the reader's problem, in their words. Use a number from their world if you have a real one.
2. **Show the need.** What does the problem cost them? A lost customer, a wasted evening, money.
3. **Show the fix.** One clear promise, as a benefit, not a feature: "patients book while they still feel the toothache", not "AI booking assistant".
4. **Prove it** with a demo, a screenshot, real numbers, a guarantee or "pay only if it works".
5. **Ask for one small next step.** In a cold message that's a reply, not a call or a price.

## The seven levers (Cialdini), used honestly

| Lever | What it means | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocation** | People repay a gift | Give something tailored first (a free audit, a sample, a useful finding), then make a small, fair ask soon after. |
| **Liking** | We say yes to people we like and who are like us | Real common ground, their own words, one specific compliment, a shared contact if there is one. Frame it as "us against the problem". |
| **Social proof** | People follow what others like them do | Real reviews, numbers, logos. With no customers yet, show proof of work (a live demo, a real result) and an honest trend. Don't say "most people fail at X": it makes failing sound normal. Say what the best do. |
| **Authority** | People defer to credible experts | Admit a weakness first, then give the strongest point: "We're new, so you only pay if it works." Borrow authority only from real sources. |
| **Scarcity** | People want what's limited, and fear losing more than they like gaining | Only real limits ("3 spots this month" when it's true), framed as loss. Never fake a deadline or a count. |
| **Commitment and consistency** | People act in line with what they've already said and done | A small-yes ladder: an easy question, their own number, a free test, then the offer. Never change terms after a yes. |
| **Unity** | We favour people who are "one of us" | Real shared identity: same trade, same city, same fight. Ask for their "advice", not their "opinion". |

**Add a reason.** Every ask gets a true "because". **Offer a way out.** "You're free to say no" often raises yes rates.

**The ethical line.** Use a lever only when the fact behind it is real. If the reader could later find out the reason, the scarcity or the proof was false, don't use it.

## Formats

### Cold email
- **Subject:** short and specific. Score it on the 4 U's (urgent, unique, ultra-specific, useful). Rewrite if it fails two.
- **The first two lines carry the message:** what you noticed about them, plus one specific idea.
- **No self-introduction.** Don't open with "I'm [name]" or "My name is". The name is at the bottom. Greet them and get to the point.
- **One goal: a reply.** No price, no call request, no attachment, one link or none.
- **Under 100 words.** End with a short P.S. or a "not now is fine" line. Plain text, an opt-out line, no tracking. Keep volume low from a new domain.

### DM (X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Telegram, WhatsApp)
- Show you looked at their business (Tyson 4D). One idea, one question, under 60 words.

### Sales page or landing page
- One job, so no menu.
- Trust on the first screen.
- The guarantee next to the button.
- Every claim proved.
- Section order: see `landing-pages-and-websites.md`.

### Social post and short video
- Hook in the first line or first 2 seconds, about the reader's problem.
- One topic. Show it working. Captions on. One next step.

### Flyer or leave-behind
- A selling headline on the front, not the company name.
- Subheads that tell the whole pitch to someone who only skims.
- A caption on every picture, the price, the guarantee and one next step.

### Follow-ups
- Give before you ask: one or two useful messages, then the offer with a real deadline.
- Match where the reader came from (Georgi: "Context is king").

### Curiosity bullets (Georgi)
- Short lines that tease a specific payoff keep a skimmer reading. Keep them true.

## Headlines and hooks
- Write 4-5 options of different types and score each on the 4 U's.
- Lead with self-interest or news.
- Be specific: numbers, places, times.
- Use the buyer's words.
- Believable beats clever.

All the types, with examples, are in `headlines.md`.

## The 10-point check (run before anything goes out)
1. Does it open with their problem, in their words?
2. Does every sentence pass the three tests?
3. Is each feature turned into what they gain?
4. Is there one clear promise and real proof?
5. Is every lever backed by a true fact?
6. Is there exactly one next step, sized for where they are (reply, test, call, buy)?
7. Is it short? Cold emails under 100 words, DMs under 60.
8. Could a 12-year-old read it? Plain words, short sentences.
9. Are all unproven numbers and claims still in [brackets]?
10. For email: plain text, an opt-out line, low volume.

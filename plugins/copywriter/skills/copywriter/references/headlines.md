# Headlines, subject lines and hooks

Based on Robert W. Bly's The Copywriter's Handbook (4th ed.), in our own words, plus ideas credited inline.

Use this file whenever you write the first thing a reader sees: a headline, an email subject line, a DM preview, a post hook, a flyer top or the opening seconds of a video.

## Why the headline matters most

Most people read the first line and stop. So the headline does most of the selling. A good one promises the reader something they want, or tells them something new. A pun that hides the benefit wastes the whole piece.

John Caples, quoted by Bly, said the best headlines "appeal to people's self-interest, or give news."

## Where the first impression is, by format

- Cold email: sender name and subject line, then the first sentence.
- DM or chat message: the preview line shown before it is opened.
- Landing page: the top headline and the line just under it.
- Flyer or one-pager: the top third of the page.
- Video or short-form post: the first 2-3 seconds.
- Paper letter: the envelope, then the opening line.

Treat each of these as a headline and apply every rule below.

## The four jobs a headline can do

1. **Stop the reader.** Promise a benefit, share news or offer something free.
2. **Pick the right reader.** Name who it is for, so the wrong people skip it and the right people lean in.
3. **Sell on its own.** Many people read nothing else, so put the full promise in it. Add the product name if they may not read on.
4. **Pull them into the body.** Raise a question they want answered.

A strong headline does at least two of these. The best do all four.

## Headline types

The first 8 are the basic types. The rest are extra angles to try when the basics feel flat. Pick 3-4 types that suit the offer and write a line for each.

1. **Direct.** Say the offer plainly.
   Example: "Online bookkeeping for small shops. [$49] a month."
2. **Indirect or curiosity.** A line with a twist or double meaning that the body explains.
   Example: "The one receipt that costs most cafe owners [$600] a year"
3. **News.** Announce something new or newly possible.
   Example: "New: book a dental cleaning from your phone in [30] seconds"
4. **How-to.** Promise a useful method. When stuck, start with "How to" and finish the line.
   Example: "How to run your first 5K without getting shin splints"
5. **Question.** Ask something the reader already wonders. Ask about them, never about you.
   Example: "Who answers your support emails at 9pm on a Saturday?"
6. **Command.** Open with a strong verb that tells the reader what to do.
   Example: "Stop typing invoices by hand this month"
7. **Reason-why or numbered list.** A number, then "reasons" or "ways", then the list in the body.
   Example: "7 reasons new runners quit in week 3"
8. **Testimonial.** A real customer's words in quote marks, edited lightly if at all.
   Example: "'I got my evenings back.' [Name], owner of [a bakery]" (only with a real quote)
9. **Call out the reader.** Name who it is for in the first words.
   Example: "Freelance designers: a quick question about late invoices"
10. **Name the problem.** Lead with the pain the reader feels.
    Example: "Your team spends Friday chasing timesheets"
11. **Offer or guarantee.** Lead with the deal or the removal of risk.
    Example: "Try it free for 30 days. Cancel with one click."
12. **Price.** Lead with a low or clear price.
    Example: "Less than [$2] a day to hit every payroll deadline"
13. **Specific numbers.** Exact figures, written as digits, to build belief.
    Example: "[312] clinics cut no-shows by [37%] in [90] days"
14. **Free resource.** Offer a useful guide, checklist or report.
    Example: "Free: the 10-minute month-end checklist for shop owners"
15. **Comparison.** Show a better way than the usual one.
    Example: "Cheaper than a part-time bookkeeper, and it never takes a holiday"
16. **Contradiction ("X without Y").** Promise the gain without the usual cost.
    Example: "Train for a half marathon without giving up your weekends"
17. **Return on money.** Show what it earns or saves.
    Example: "One extra booking a week pays for a year of [the app]"
18. **Inside info or secret.** Tease knowledge others lack.
    Example: "The tax deduction most freelancers forget to claim"
19. **Story.** Open a short narrative.
    Example: "I emailed 10 software vendors on a Sunday. Here is who replied." (only if you did it)
20. **Urgency or time.** Add a real deadline or a reason to act now.
    Example: "[3] onboarding spots left for March" (only if true)
21. **Answer buyer questions.** Promise straight answers to the questions people ask before buying.
    Example: "4 questions to ask before you switch accounting software"
22. **New term.** Coin a name for your method.
    Example: "Batch booking: fill a whole week of appointments in one message"

You can also tie a headline to something in the news, if the link is real and the reader cares about it.

## The 4 U's test (credit: Mark Ford)

Score every headline from 1 (weak) to 4 (strong) on each U:

1. **Urgent.** Is there a reason to act now, such as a time frame or a deadline?
2. **Unique.** Is it new, or said in a fresh way?
3. **Ultra-specific.** Does it use exact numbers, names or details?
4. **Useful.** Does it offer a clear benefit to the reader?

How to use the scores:

- Write the four scores next to each draft, for example "U 2, U 3, U 4, U 4".
- Keep a headline only if it scores 3 or more on at least three of the four U's.
- If it fails, raise the lowest score first. Add a number, a time frame or a sharper benefit.
- Use the same test on subject lines, DM openers, flyer tops and subheads.

Worked example:

- Before: "Software for clinics" scores urgent 1, unique 1, specific 1, useful 2. Rewrite.
- After: "Fill tomorrow's empty dental slots by 5pm today" scores 3, 3, 3, 4. Keep.

## How to find a headline (Bly's process)

1. Answer four questions in writing:
   - Who is the buyer?
   - What are the key features?
   - Which features do rivals lack?
   - Which benefit matters most to the buyer?
2. The answer to "which features do rivals lack" is the main selling point. Put it in the headline.
3. Write 6-8 versions using different types from the list above.
4. Score them with the 4 U's. Pick the winner.
5. Keep the runners-up. Use them as subheads, bullets or follow-up subject lines.
6. If stuck, list 20 words linked to the product and mix them into lines.
7. If still stuck, write the body first. The headline is often hiding in it.
8. Study ads and emails that keep running for months. Repeats usually mean they make money. Borrow the pattern, never the words.

## Quick rules

1. Promise a benefit or a reward for reading.
2. Be clear first. Be clever only if it makes the benefit clearer.
3. Be as specific as you can. Use digits for numbers.
4. Make the headline match the product. Bait and switch makes readers angry.
5. If there is a picture, make the headline and the picture tell one story.
6. Avoid "blind" headlines that mean nothing without the body.
7. State things positively. "100% on time" beats "never late".
8. Do not avoid common power words such as free, new, how to, you, save, proven and easy. They are common because they work.
9. Only claim what is true today. Put any number you do not have yet in [brackets] until a real figure replaces it.
10. Never ask a question about yourself or your company. Ask about the reader.

## Check

1. Does it promise a clear benefit or real news?
2. Does it name or clearly pick out the right reader?
3. Would it still sell if the reader saw nothing else?
4. Does it score 3 or more on at least three of the 4 U's?
5. Is it plain, specific and true, with no pun hiding the point?
